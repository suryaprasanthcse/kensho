### What is known about the 429s

This is not from the tapes. The owner read it in AI Studio on 2026-11-01 at 19:08 IST: `gemini-3.8-flash` has a limit of 20 requests per day (RPD) on the Free tier. That is **consistent with a daily request quota** as the cause of the 429s of `gemini-3.8-flash`: 16 replies were served before the first 429, and the model had been called earlier the same day. It is **not shown to be the cause**: the study never read the body of a 429. An amendment to finish the generations was withdrawn because 20 requests a day is fewer than the 37 to 74 it needed (one request per missing generation, plus at most one repair each), and the owner chose not to depend on a change of billing tier or on a run spread over several days.

## 2. Exploratory analyses: NOT pre-registered

**Both analyses below were chosen after the pre-registered results were seen. They are not part of the pre-registration, have no multiplicity adjustment, and must not be read as confirmatory.** They are here to describe the data, not to test anything.

### E1. gemini-3.5-flash-lite on the pairs where gemini-3.8-flash produced a scene

The comparison in section 1 is not like for like, because `gemini-3.8-flash` only ran the first prompts. This restricts `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` to exactly the (prompt_id, sample) pairs where `gemini-3.8-flash` produced a scene (a generation that was not a transport failure): 16 pairs, ord-01 (3), ord-02 (3), ord-03 (3), ord-04 (3), ord-05 (3), ord-06 (1).

| Model | On those pairs: fail / schema-valid scenes |
|---|---|
| gemini-3.5-flash-lite | 14/15 = 93.3% (95% CI 70.2% to 98.8%) (not counted: 0 schema_invalid, 1 transport failures, 0 with no tape) |
| gemini-3.8-flash | 0/16 = 0.0% (95% CI 0.0% to 19.4%) |

The pairs are the earliest requests of `gemini-3.8-flash` in the file order (all ordinary prompts) and the ones the provider happened to serve before it began refusing requests; they are not a random sample. With 16 scenes the intervals are wide, and `gemini-3.8-flash` and `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` also differ in default thinking.

### E2. gemini-3.5-flash-lite excluding OUT_OF_BOUNDS

Every failing scene of `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` has `OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (section 1, D1). This counts a scene as failing only if it has at least one error code other than `OUT_OF_BOUNDS`, that is, the failure rate if `OUT_OF_BOUNDS` were not an error.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Fail with all ten checks (pre-registered M1) | 49/57 = 86.0% (95% CI 74.7% to 92.7%) |
| Fail with OUT_OF_BOUNDS ignored | 15/57 = 26.3% (95% CI 16.6% to 39.0%) |
| Failing scenes whose only error code is OUT_OF_BOUNDS | 34 of 49 |
| Other codes among failing scenes (scenes that have each) | SPATIAL_OVERLAP 15, SPAWN_OBSTRUCTED 2 |

`OUT_OF_BOUNDS` fires when any box, pickups included, extends outside `bounds_mm`. The ground is not a solid in these checks, so a floor slab centred below y = 0 inside bounds that start at y = 0 is flagged. This analysis shows how much of the failure rate rests on that one rule.

## 3. Limits

- The checks are my own, on my own format; a failing scene is not shown to be unplayable in any engine, and a passing one is not shown to be playable. No human-labelled sample was made.
- `gemini-3.8-flash` has 23 of 60 generations, and every failed attempt of its transport failures was HTTP 429 (see "What is known about the 429s" in section 1), so nothing about its scenes should be read from section 1 beyond what the tapes contain.
- Sampling and thinking were the providers' defaults, which differ between the two models.
